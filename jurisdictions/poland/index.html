Executive Summary

Real estate law in Poland is the legal and administrative framework governing ownership, transfer, registration, financing, leasing, perpetual usufruct, use and development of land and buildings. It is characterised by mandatory notarial form for transfers of real property and a public Land and Mortgage Register system, księga wieczysta, maintained through district courts.

A Polish property transaction commonly involves review of the Land and Mortgage Register, land-and-building information, title documents, planning status, lease arrangements and transaction documentation. A contract transferring ownership of real property must be executed as a notarial deed. The notary commonly prepares the deed and submits the application for registration of the resulting rights.

Land and Mortgage Registers are public and can be consulted electronically using the register number. The register is designed to establish the legal status of real estate and records ownership, perpetual usufruct, limited rights, claims and mortgages. A separate Land and Building Register, ewidencja gruntów i budynków, provides cadastral and descriptive property information.

Cross-border transactions require coordination of Polish notarial, register, tax, planning and foreign-investment considerations with foreign buyer, lender and group requirements. International parties should assess corporate authority, acquisition permissions where applicable, financing security, tax, anti-money-laundering rules, building compliance and the correct registration process.

Object Definition

Real estate law in Poland is the professional legal function through which rights in land and buildings are acquired, documented, reviewed, notarised, registered, financed, leased, developed and transferred. It links contractual arrangements with the Polish notarial system, district-court Land and Mortgage Registers, cadastral information and planning controls.

Scope

The Registry Object covers the operating framework for Polish real-property matters. It includes acquisition, sale, notarial formalities, land-and-mortgage register review, cadastral information, mortgages, perpetual usufruct, leases, planning and development, while recognising that complex transactions require coordinated tax, environmental, construction, corporate and financing work.

Covered MattersProperty acquisitions and sales, preliminary agreements, notarial deeds, Land and Mortgage Register review and applications, Land and Building Register information, mortgages, easements, perpetual usufruct, leases, planning and building issues, development arrangements and real-estate financing.

Purpose

The purpose of the real-estate law function is to establish a reliable legal basis for acquiring, holding, financing, using, leasing or developing Polish property. It converts commercial arrangements into valid notarial documentation and an appropriately registered property position capable of being relied upon by parties, lenders and third parties.

For an investor, owner or occupier, this requires analysis of the Land and Mortgage Register, land-and-building information, title documents, mortgages, easements, perpetual usufruct, leases, planning conditions, tax treatment and authority processes affecting the property.

Country Characteristics

Poland has two complementary property-information systems. The Land and Mortgage Register, księga wieczysta, is a public court register intended to establish the legal status of real property. The Land and Building Register, ewidencja gruntów i budynków, provides descriptive data concerning land, buildings and premises. These systems should be considered together in legal due diligence.

Institutional StructureDistrict courts maintain Land and Mortgage Registers, supported by a central electronic database maintained under the Ministry of Justice framework.
Notarial EnvironmentA sale agreement transferring real property must be executed in the form of a notarial deed. The notary prepares the act and commonly submits registration applications.
Land and Mortgage RegisterThe public register records the legal status of real property, including ownership, perpetual usufruct, limited rights, claims and mortgages.
Land and Building RegisterThe cadastral register contains descriptive and technical data about land, buildings and premises and is maintained through the relevant local administrative system.
Registration EffectFor an ordinary property sale, transfer occurs through the notarial agreement; entry of the new owner is generally declaratory, while registration is constitutive for certain rights such as a mortgage.
Planning EnvironmentDevelopment may require a local spatial development plan, zoning decision, building permit and, in appropriate cases, environmental approvals.
Language ExpectationPolish is central to notarial, court, registry, tax and public processes. English is common in international transactions but does not replace Polish formal requirements.

Process Flow

A Polish property transaction is a coordinated due-diligence, notarial, tax and registration process. The legal and cadastral position is reviewed, preliminary terms are agreed where applicable, the final sale deed is executed before a notary, taxes and court fees are managed, and the application for Land and Mortgage Register entry is submitted.

1. Property and Register ReviewConfirm property identity, Land and Mortgage Register number, ownership, mortgages, easements, perpetual usufruct, claims, land-and-building information and relevant discrepancies.
2. Wider Due DiligenceReview leases, planning and building status, permits, technical matters, environmental exposure, tax and commercial risks relevant to the asset.
3. Preliminary DocumentationNegotiate preliminary agreement, conditions, financing and transaction timetable where appropriate.
4. Notarial PreparationThe notary reviews required documents, prepares the deed, verifies parties and authority, and coordinates tax, fees and registration filings.
5. Notarial Deed and CompletionThe parties sign the transfer deed before the notary; payment, delivery and agreed completion actions are carried out.
6. Registration and Follow-UpThe notary or relevant party submits the Land and Mortgage Register application; related mortgage, cadastral, tax or planning follow-up is completed.

Decision Tree

  1. Is the matter an acquisition, sale, lease, financing, development, perpetual-usufruct or dispute issue?
  2. What does the Land and Mortgage Register show about ownership, mortgages, rights, claims and encumbrances?
  3. What does the Land and Building Register show about the property description, boundaries, buildings and premises?
  4. Does the local spatial plan, zoning decision, building permit or environmental process affect the intended use?
  5. Is a preliminary agreement required, and which financing, due-diligence, permit or condition-precedent provisions should it contain?
  6. Which notarial deed, mortgage document or Land and Mortgage Register application must be prepared?
  7. Have tax, corporate authority, acquisition-permission, foreign documentation and compliance requirements been incorporated into the transaction structure?

Cross-Border Relevance

Poland is an important destination for international logistics, industrial, office, residential and development investment. Foreign investors and lenders can participate, but Polish notarial, register, tax, planning and acquisition requirements remain decisive. Early coordination is needed where foreign corporate records, financing, acquisition permissions, powers of attorney, translations and compliance arrangements are involved.

RecognitionRights in Polish real property are governed by Polish law and are recorded or protected through the applicable notarial and Land and Mortgage Register framework.
Foreign CompaniesForeign purchasers and lenders may need Polish tax or registration identifiers, corporate extracts, authority evidence, powers of attorney, legalisation, translations and compliance documentation appropriate to the transaction.
Language ConsiderationsPolish is central to notarial, court, registry, tax and public processes. English can support commercial negotiations but does not replace the Polish language requirements of formal acts.
International RulesEU rules, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, tax, financing and group-governance requirements may influence the wider transaction structure.
Typical RisksAssuming that a private contract, unreviewed register entry or foreign security document alone provides adequate protection without the required Polish notarial and registration steps.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Polish Land and Mortgage Register?The księga wieczysta is a public register maintained by district courts to establish the legal status of real property, including ownership, rights, claims and mortgages.
Can it be checked online?Yes. Land and Mortgage Registers can be consulted electronically by entering the register number.
Is a notarial deed required to buy Polish real estate?Yes. A contract transferring ownership of real property must be executed as a notarial deed.
What is the Land and Building Register?The ewidencja gruntów i budynków is a separate cadastral system containing descriptive information on land, buildings and premises.
When is registration constitutive?For an ordinary sale, ownership generally transfers on signing the notarial deed. A mortgage, however, becomes effective upon registration in the Land and Mortgage Register.

Practical Guidance

Before acquiring, financing or developing Polish property, obtain a current Land and Mortgage Register extract and land-and-building information, then compare them with the physical and contractual position. The notarial deed, register application, planning status, financing security, tax treatment, foreign-party documentation and corporate authority should be managed as one coordinated transaction workstream.

Preparation checklist: Confirm property and register number; review the Land and Mortgage Register and Land and Building Register; identify mortgages, easements, perpetual usufruct, leases and claims; review planning and permit status; determine the transaction structure; verify signing authority and foreign-party documentation; prepare the notarial deed; map tax, court and registration actions; and align legal, tax, technical and financing workstreams.
