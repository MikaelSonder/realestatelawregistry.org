Executive Summary

Real estate law in Ontario is the provincial and local legal framework governing ownership, transfer, registration, financing, leasing, use and development of land and buildings. It operates through Ontario’s electronic land-registration system, provincial property and contract law, municipal planning and zoning powers, provincial tax rules and a conveyancing process commonly coordinated by lawyers, title insurers, lenders and licensed real estate professionals.

An Ontario property transaction normally requires review of the parcel register, registered instruments, title evidence, easements, restrictive covenants, mortgages, executions, leases, surveys, tax status, zoning, planning and transaction documents. The transfer is generally registered electronically through Teraview in Ontario’s Electronic Land Registration System. Registration is central to the transfer, priority and enforceability of registered land interests.

Ontario’s Land Registration System is administered through Land Registry Offices and is supported by electronic infrastructure including Teraview, OnLand, Teranet eXpress and the POLARIS title database. Ontario’s system includes both Land Titles and Registry Act land. Most current transactions use electronic registration through authorised professionals. A complete review can require a parcel register search, instrument review, plan review, execution search and municipal due diligence.

Ontario transactions are closely connected to provincial Land Transfer Tax. In Toronto, a separate Municipal Land Transfer Tax can also apply. Foreign buyers of residential property must assess Ontario’s Non-Resident Speculation Tax, or NRST, which is generally a 25 percent tax on qualifying residential acquisitions by foreign entities or taxable trustees. The federal Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act can also restrict certain acquisitions of residential property. These tax, eligibility and registration issues should be resolved before closing.

It includes acquisition, sale, title and parcel-register review, Land Registry Office filing, Teraview registration, Land Transfer Tax, NRST, mortgages, executions, easements, leases, condominium property, planning, zoning and development, while recognising that complex matters require coordinated tax, environmental, construction, corporate, regulatory and financing work.

Purpose

The purpose of the real-estate law function is to establish a reliable legal basis for acquiring, holding, financing, using, leasing or developing Ontario property. It converts commercial terms into enforceable agreements, completed conveyancing documents, correctly filed Land Transfer Tax and NRST materials, and registered land interests supported by title evidence, title insurance and appropriate municipal, lender and regulatory compliance.

For an investor, owner or occupier, this requires analysis of parcel registers, registered instruments, title notices, mortgages, executions, easements, restrictive covenants, leases, condominium documents, survey and plans, planning and zoning records, environmental matters, property tax, Land Transfer Tax, NRST and the appropriate electronic registration route.

Ontario Characteristics

Ontario real estate is defined by a province-wide electronic land-registration environment, lawyer-led conveyancing, title insurance, Land Transfer Tax and municipal planning powers. Land Registry Offices maintain official title records, while licensed professionals access and submit registrations through Teraview. OnLand provides public access to available title and instrument information, and Teranet eXpress provides self-service access to selected Land Registration and Writs System data.

Land Registration EnvironmentOntario’s Land Registration System records official property titles, parcel registers, instruments and plans through Land Registry Offices. The system includes Land Titles and Registry Act land, with most contemporary transactions completed electronically.
Teraview EnvironmentTeraview is Ontario’s secure electronic land-registration system for authorised users. It enables title and writ searches, viewing of instruments and plans, preparation of electronic documents and submission of registrations.
OnLand EnvironmentOnLand provides public access to information available from Ontario’s Land Registration System, including parcel registers, instruments, historical books and plans available through the service.
POLARIS EnvironmentPOLARIS, the Province of Ontario Land Registration Information System, contains official automated land-registration data and supports title, registration and property-maintenance functions.
Registration EffectOntario registration is central to title, priority and enforceability. Registered title, instruments, notices and charges should be reviewed together with applicable land-registration legislation, title evidence, survey and municipal information.
Conveyancing EnvironmentTransactions commonly use an agreement of purchase and sale, title and execution searches, survey, title insurance, closing documents, Land Transfer Tax statements, mortgage documents, electronic funds flow and Teraview registration.
Land Transfer Tax EnvironmentOntario Land Transfer Tax is payable on qualifying transfers of land. Provincial land transfer tax statements are incorporated into Teraview. Toronto transactions can also attract Municipal Land Transfer Tax.
NRST EnvironmentOntario’s Non-Resident Speculation Tax applies to qualifying acquisitions of residential property by foreign entities and taxable trustees. It is generally payable at closing when a transfer is registered, subject to statutory exemptions, rebates and transitional rules.
Planning and Development EnvironmentMunicipalities regulate land use through official plans, zoning by-laws, site-plan control, subdivision, condominium, minor variance, consent and building-permit processes under Ontario’s planning and building legislation.
Language ExpectationEnglish is the standard language for land registration, conveyancing, taxation, municipal planning and court processes. French-language rights and services can apply in designated circumstances. Foreign documents may require notarisation, apostille, certification or translation acceptable to the relevant authority, lender or transaction party.

Process Flow

An Ontario property transaction is a coordinated title, agreement, due-diligence, financing, Land Transfer Tax, NRST, closing and electronic registration process. The property, Land Registry Office and municipal jurisdiction are identified; title and due diligence are completed; transaction and financing documents are negotiated; tax and foreign-buyer analysis is completed; closing occurs; and the transfer and charge are registered electronically through the applicable Ontario system.

1. Property and Registration IdentificationConfirm municipality, Land Registry Office or registration division, PIN or property identifier, parcel register, legal description, title reference, zoning, property tax and applicable transaction structure.
2. Title, Writ, Survey and Property ReviewReview parcel register, instruments, charges, mortgages, notices, easements, covenants, leases, execution and writ searches, survey, plan, tax status, condominium records and local authority information.
3. Wider Due DiligenceReview leases, condominium status certificate and governing documents, zoning, official plan, permits, environmental matters, building condition, conservation authority constraints, utilities, tax, insurance and commercial risks.
4. Agreement and Financing DocumentationNegotiate agreement of purchase and sale, closing documents, financing and charge documents, representations, warranties, indemnities, disclosure materials, conditions, title insurance and settlement mechanics.
5. Land Transfer Tax, NRST and ClosingDetermine Ontario Land Transfer Tax, Toronto Municipal Land Transfer Tax where applicable, NRST and federal foreign-buyer eligibility; prepare declarations and statements; execute transfer, mortgage and closing materials; complete funding, discharge of existing charges, possession and settlement.
6. Electronic Registration and Follow-UpRegister the transfer, charge, discharge, notice, easement, plan or other instruments electronically through Teraview or the applicable registration process; then complete title insurance, tax, lender reporting, lease, planning and post-closing actions.

Decision Tree

  1. In which Ontario municipality and Land Registry Office registration division is the property located?
  2. What do the parcel register, registered instruments, writ and execution searches, survey, plan, assessment and municipal records show about ownership, charges, mortgages, easements, covenants, leases and exceptions?
  3. Is the property Land Titles land, Registry Act land, residential, commercial, industrial, agricultural, condominium, mixed-use, development land or subject to another specialised regime?
  4. Do the official plan, zoning by-law, site-plan-control process, subdivision or condominium approval, conservation authority regulations, environmental constraints, building permit or condominium requirements affect the intended use?
  5. Does Ontario Land Transfer Tax apply? Is Toronto Municipal Land Transfer Tax applicable? Is NRST payable, exempt or eligible for a rebate?
  6. Does the federal Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act apply to the purchaser, property type or transaction structure?
  7. Which agreement, transfer, charge, discharge, title insurance, Land Transfer Tax statements, NRST declarations, execution documents and electronic registration materials are required?
  8. Does the transaction involve a foreign seller, foreign buyer, foreign corporation, taxable trustee, nonresident tax, GST/HST, sanctions, beneficial ownership, financing or other cross-border requirements?

Frequently Asked Questions

Who registers ownership of property in Ontario?Ontario Land Registry Offices maintain official land-registration records. Most current registration work is completed electronically by authorised professionals through Teraview in Ontario’s Electronic Land Registration System.
What is a parcel register?A parcel register is the official record for a registered property interest. It identifies the property, PIN, registered owners, instruments, charges, notices, easements, restrictions and other title matters.
What are Teraview and OnLand?Teraview is the secure system used by authorised professionals to search, prepare and register Ontario land documents electronically. OnLand is a public-facing service for searching available Ontario land-registration records, including parcel registers, documents and plans.
What is Ontario Land Transfer Tax?Ontario Land Transfer Tax is a provincial tax on qualifying land transfers. Provincial statements are incorporated into Teraview. Toronto transactions can additionally be subject to Municipal Land Transfer Tax.
What is NRST?NRST is Ontario’s Non-Resident Speculation Tax. It is generally a 25 percent tax on qualifying acquisitions of residential property by foreign entities or taxable trustees, subject to legislation, exemptions, rebates and transitional provisions.
Can a non-Canadian buy Ontario residential property?Eligibility must be assessed carefully. Ontario NRST can apply to qualifying foreign buyers, and the federal Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act can prohibit or restrict certain purchases of residential property by non-Canadians, subject to exceptions and current regulations.
Are planning and building permits provincial or municipal?Planning, zoning, subdivision, site-plan and many development approvals are principally administered by municipalities under the Planning Act. Building permits and inspections are administered through municipal building departments under the Building Code Act and Ontario Building Code.

Practical Guidance

Before acquiring, financing or developing Ontario property, identify the municipality, Land Registry Office, PIN, title system and purchaser profile first. Obtain parcel-register and instrument information, execution searches, survey and plan material, municipal tax, zoning and permit records and, where applicable condominium, environmental, conservation-authority and foreign-buyer documentation. The agreement, title insurance, Land Transfer Tax, Toronto tax, NRST, federal foreign-buyer review, financing, closing and electronic registration should be managed as one coordinated workstream.

Preparation checklist: Confirm the Ontario municipality, Land Registry Office and PIN; obtain the parcel register, registered instruments, plan and execution searches; identify charges, mortgages, liens, notices, easements, covenants, leases, condominium obligations and restrictions; review assessment, property tax, official plan, zoning, subdivision, site-plan, conservation authority, environmental, permit and building status; determine the transaction and finance structure; verify signing authority, foreign-party documentation, beneficial ownership, AML and source-of-funds information; assess Ontario Land Transfer Tax, Toronto Municipal Land Transfer Tax where applicable, NRST and federal foreign-buyer eligibility; prepare agreement, transfer, charge, tax, title insurance and Teraview registration materials; and align legal, tax, technical, planning, lender and post-closing workstreams.
