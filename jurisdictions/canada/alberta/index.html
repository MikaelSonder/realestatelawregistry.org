Executive Summary

Real estate law in Alberta is the provincial and local legal framework governing ownership, transfer, registration, financing, leasing, use and development of land and buildings. It operates through Alberta’s Torrens-based Land Titles system, provincial property and contract law, municipal and regional planning powers, local property taxation and a conveyancing process commonly coordinated by lawyers, lenders, real estate professionals and title or registration service providers.

An Alberta property transaction normally requires review of the certificate of title, registered encumbrances, mortgages, caveats, easements, restrictive covenants, leases, surveys, plans, tax status, zoning, planning and transaction documents. Ownership changes are made through a Transfer of Land form under the Land Titles Act and are registered with Alberta Land Titles. Registration is central to the creation, transfer, priority and enforceability of registered land interests under Alberta’s Torrens system.

Alberta Land Titles is part of the provincial registry system and operates through offices in Edmonton and Calgary that function as one operation. Alberta’s land-title records, registered documents and survey plans can be searched through provincial online land registry services. The system maintains title, registered interest, survey and historical title records; a complete property review requires title, instrument, plan, municipal, tax, environmental and financing due diligence.

Alberta does not levy a percentage-based provincial land transfer tax comparable to those in several other Canadian provinces. Instead, Alberta Land Titles charges registration fees based on a base charge plus a value-based levy for transfers and mortgages. Foreign ownership of land is subject to the Foreign Ownership of Land Regulations, which can restrict acquisition of controlled land by non-Canadian citizens, permanent residents and certain foreign-controlled corporations. Foreign buyer transactions also require assessment of federal residential-property purchase restrictions, GST, income tax, sanctions, beneficial ownership and financing requirements.

It converts commercial terms into enforceable agreements, completed transfer and mortgage documents, properly registered title interests and a property position supported by certificate-of-title evidence, survey and plan information, lender requirements and appropriate local compliance.

Alberta Characteristics

Alberta real estate is defined by its Torrens land-title system, title registration through Alberta Land Titles, use of certificates of title, value-based registration fees instead of a general land transfer tax, local-government development control and a major interaction between surface property, Crown lands and separately owned mineral interests. Alberta Land Titles maintains provincial records through Edmonton and Calgary offices operating as one system.

Torrens Title EnvironmentAlberta uses a Torrens-based land-title system. Alberta Land Titles registers land ownership, transfers, mortgages, caveats, liens, easements, restrictive covenants and other interests affecting title under the Land Titles Act.
Certificate of Title EnvironmentA certificate of title identifies the registered owner, legal land description, registered encumbrances, mortgages, caveats, easements, restrictive covenants and other title information. It should be reviewed with all relevant registered instruments and plans.
Land Titles Office EnvironmentAlberta has Land Titles offices in Edmonton and Calgary. The offices function as one operation and accept registration packages, title searches, survey plans and other land-title services for Alberta property.
Transfer of Land EnvironmentTransfers of land are regulated under the Land Titles Act. Except for special ownership changes, a prescribed Transfer of Land form is required to change registered ownership, showing transferor, transferee, legal description, consideration, value and ownership form.
Registration EffectRegistration is central to legal title, priority and enforceability. A transfer, mortgage, caveat, lien, discharge, easement or other registrable interest must be correctly registered to obtain the intended land-title effect.
Survey and Plan EnvironmentLand Titles and Survey records include survey plans, subdivision plans, reference plans, condominium plans and other spatial records relevant to legal land description, boundaries, access and property interests.
Fees Rather Than Transfer TaxAlberta does not impose a general percentage-based provincial land transfer tax. Instead, Land Titles charges transfer and mortgage registration fees calculated using a base fee and value-based levy under the current fee schedule.
Foreign Ownership EnvironmentThe Foreign Ownership of Land Regulations can restrict acquisition of controlled land by non-Canadian citizens, permanent residents and certain foreign-controlled corporations. Controlled land generally includes land outside specified urban areas, subject to statutory definitions, exemptions and limits.
Planning and Development EnvironmentMunicipalities, counties and municipal districts regulate land use through statutory plans, land-use bylaws, subdivision, development permits, building permits, environmental constraints, utility and infrastructure conditions under provincial and local law.
Language ExpectationEnglish is the standard language for Land Titles, conveyancing, taxation, planning and court processes. Foreign documents may require notarisation, apostille, legalisation, certification or translation acceptable to Land Titles, lenders, authorities and transaction parties.

Process Flow

An Alberta property transaction is a coordinated title, contract, due-diligence, financing, foreign ownership, closing and Land Titles registration process. The property, title and local jurisdiction are identified; title and due diligence are completed; transaction and financing documents are negotiated; ownership restrictions and tax are assessed; closing occurs; and the Transfer of Land and mortgage or other interests are registered with Alberta Land Titles.

1. Property and Title IdentificationConfirm municipality, county or municipal district, legal land description, title number, certificate of title, property tax, land-use district, development context and applicable transaction structure.
2. Title, Encumbrance, Survey and Property ReviewReview certificate of title, registered instruments, mortgages, caveats, liens, easements, restrictive covenants, leases, plans, surveys, mineral and surface rights where relevant, assessment, tax status and municipal records.
3. Wider Due DiligenceReview leases, condominium documents, zoning and statutory plans, development permits, environmental and water matters, building condition, utilities, access, agricultural restrictions, energy interests, tax, insurance and commercial risks.
4. Agreement and Financing DocumentationNegotiate agreement of purchase and sale, title requirements, closing documents, financing and mortgage documents, representations, warranties, indemnities, disclosure materials, conditions, title insurance and settlement mechanics.
5. Foreign Ownership, Tax and ClosingAssess Foreign Ownership of Land Regulations, federal foreign-buyer eligibility, GST and nonresident tax where relevant; prepare Transfer of Land form, affidavits, mortgage and closing materials; complete funding, discharge of existing instruments, possession and settlement.
6. Land Titles Registration and Follow-UpRegister the Transfer of Land, mortgage, discharge, caveat withdrawal, easement, restrictive covenant, plan or other instrument with Alberta Land Titles; then complete title insurance, tax, lender reporting, lease, planning and post-closing actions.

Costs and Fees

Cost analysis should distinguish Alberta Land Titles registration fees, legal and closing work, title insurance, property-tax matters, lender costs, surveys, environmental review, condominium fees and project-specific planning or development expenses. Alberta does not generally charge a percentage-based provincial land transfer tax, but registration fees are calculated on a base amount plus a value-based levy under the current fee schedule.

Land Titles Registration FeesFor a Transfer of Land or transfer of leasehold title, Alberta Land Titles charges a base fee plus a levy calculated by reference to property value. Current guidance identifies a $50 base fee and $5 per $5,000 of value, subject to current fee rules, additional-title charges and transaction-specific requirements.
Mortgage Registration FeesMortgage registration generally attracts a base fee plus a value-based levy related to principal amount under the current Land Titles fee schedule.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who registers ownership of property in Alberta?Alberta Land Titles registers ownership and interests in Alberta land. The province operates Land Titles offices in Edmonton and Calgary, functioning as one provincial land-title operation.
What is a certificate of title?A certificate of title is the official title record for Alberta land. It identifies the legal land description, registered owner and registered interests such as mortgages, caveats, easements, restrictive covenants and legal notations.
What form transfers ownership of Alberta land?Except for special ownership changes, a prescribed Transfer of Land form is required under the Land Titles Act. It must identify current owners, new owners, legal description, consideration, value, ownership form and execution information.
Does Alberta have a land transfer tax?Alberta does not generally impose a percentage-based provincial land transfer tax. Alberta Land Titles instead charges registration fees for transfers and mortgages, including a base fee and value-based levy under the current fee schedule.
Can foreign persons buy Alberta land?Foreign ownership must be assessed carefully. Alberta’s Foreign Ownership of Land Regulations can restrict acquisition of controlled land by non-Canadian citizens, permanent residents and certain foreign-controlled corporations. Federal restrictions can also apply to qualifying purchases of residential property by non-Canadians.
What is a caveat?A caveat is a registered notice claiming an interest in land. It can affect title, transferability, financing and priority. The underlying claim, registration, expiry or withdrawal position must be assessed before closing.
Are planning and building permits provincial or local?Planning, land use, subdivision and development permits are principally administered by municipalities, counties and municipal districts under Alberta law. Building permits and inspections are administered through local or accredited safety-code authorities under the applicable building framework.
