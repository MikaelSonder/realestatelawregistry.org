Executive Summary

Real estate law in Greece is the legal and administrative framework governing ownership, transfer, registration, financing, leasing, use and development of land and buildings. It is characterised by a civil-law notarial conveyancing process and a property-registration environment that is transitioning from historical local land registry records to the national Hellenic Cadastre.

A Greek property transaction normally requires review of the legal status of the property, title chain, cadastral information, mortgages, easements, planning status, tax documentation and ownership records. The final transfer is formalised before a Greek notary public by an authenticated notarial deed, after which the deed is registered with the competent Cadastre Office or, in locations not yet fully migrated, the relevant local land registry.

The Hellenic Cadastre, Ελληνικό Κτηματολόγιο, is the national digital property-registration system. In areas where cadastral registration has been completed, it records ownership, boundaries and legal rights. In other areas, the older land-registry system continues to operate until the transition is completed. The applicable local registration status must therefore be confirmed for each property.

Cross-border transactions require coordination of Greek notarial, cadastral, tax, planning and foreign-buyer requirements with the investor’s corporate, financing and compliance arrangements. International parties should address tax identification, title verification, forest and coastal restrictions, zoning, building regularity, foreign-border-area restrictions, powers of attorney and anti-money-laundering controls early in the process.

Object Definition

Real estate law in Greece is the professional legal function through which rights in land and buildings are acquired, documented, reviewed, notarised, registered, financed, leased, developed and transferred. It links private contractual arrangements with Greek notarial practice, cadastral or land-registry registration, tax formalities and planning or building controls.

The Registry Object covers the operating framework for Greek real-property matters. It includes acquisition, sale, notarial formalities, Hellenic Cadastre and land-registry review, mortgages, easements, horizontal ownership, leases, planning and development, while recognising that complex matters require coordinated tax, environmental, construction, corporate and financing work.

Purpose

The purpose of the real-estate law function is to establish a reliable legal basis for acquiring, holding, financing, using, leasing or developing Greek property. It converts commercial terms into a valid notarial transfer and an appropriately registered property position that can be relied upon by the parties, lenders and third parties.

For an investor, owner or occupier, this requires analysis of the legal title, Cadastre or land-registry status, mortgages, easements, property tax position, building regularity, planning conditions, leases and authority processes affecting the property.

Country Characteristics

Greece’s defining property-law characteristic is the coexistence of the new national Cadastre system and historical land registries during the ongoing cadastral transition. In completed cadastral areas, the Hellenic Cadastre provides an electronic register of ownership, boundaries and rights. In other areas, the older mortgage or land-registry records remain relevant. The registration authority and records must be confirmed property by property.

Institutional StructureThe Hellenic Cadastre is the national organisation responsible for the Cadastre and the progressive integration of land-registration functions.
Cadastre EnvironmentIn cadastral-operation areas, the Cadastre records legal and factual property information, including owners, boundaries, rights and related map data.
Legacy Land Registry EnvironmentIn areas where the transition has not been completed, property records may remain within the historical local land registry, hypothecary office or land-registry system.
Notarial EnvironmentA Greek notary public prepares and authenticates the transfer deed, verifies required certificates and handles core formalities before registration.
Registration EnvironmentRegistration or transcription of the notarial deed with the competent Cadastre Office or registry is essential to publicise and protect the acquisition against third parties.
Planning EnvironmentLand use and construction are affected by national planning rules, local plans, building permits, forest maps, coastline rules, archaeological or heritage constraints and municipal processes.
Language ExpectationGreek is central to notarial, Cadastre, tax, planning and public processes. English may support international transactions but does not replace Greek formal documentation.

Process Flow

A Greek property transaction is a coordinated due-diligence, notarial, tax and registration process. The legal and cadastral position must be reviewed first. The notary then prepares the deed after obtaining the necessary certificates and documents, and the signed deed is registered with the competent Cadastre Office or local registry.

1. Property and Registration-System ReviewConfirm the property identity, Cadastre code or registry reference, whether the relevant area is within the operational Cadastre, and the applicable local registration authority.
2. Title, Cadastre and Due Diligence ReviewReview ownership, title chain, mortgages, easements, claims, cadastral mapping, building regularity, leases, planning, tax and commercial risks.
3. Preliminary DocumentationNegotiate and prepare preliminary terms, deposit arrangements, financing conditions, warranties and completion timetable where appropriate.
4. Notarial PreparationThe notary collects or verifies property, tax, planning, cadastral and identity documentation, then prepares the authenticated deed.
5. Notarial Deed and CompletionThe parties sign the transfer deed before the notary; payment, delivery and agreed completion actions are carried out.
6. Registration and Follow-UpThe deed is submitted for registration or transcription at the competent Cadastre Office or land registry, and related tax, mortgage or cadastral follow-up is completed.

Decision Tree

  1. Is the matter an acquisition, sale, lease, financing, development, horizontal-property or dispute issue?
  2. Is the property located in an operational Hellenic Cadastre area or an area still served by a legacy land registry?
  3. What do the title, Cadastre or registry records show about ownership, boundaries, mortgages, easements, claims and other encumbrances?
  4. Do forest maps, coastline restrictions, archaeological rules, local planning, building permits or construction-regularity matters affect the intended use?
  5. Which tax certificates, property certificates, notarial documents and mortgage releases must be available before the deed can be signed?
  6. Which registration or transcription filing must follow the notarial transfer?
  7. Have foreign-buyer eligibility, tax identification, corporate authority, financing, translations and compliance requirements been incorporated into the transaction structure?

Cross-Border Relevance

Greece is an important destination for international residential, hospitality, tourism, logistics, commercial and development investment. Foreign parties can participate, but the applicable registration system, Greek notarial process, tax documentation, local planning rules and any restrictions on property near border areas must be resolved before completion.

RecognitionRights in Greek real property are governed by Greek law and are recorded or protected through the applicable Cadastre or land-registry registration framework.
Foreign Companies and IndividualsForeign purchasers and lenders may need Greek tax identification, corporate extracts, authority evidence, powers of attorney, legalisation, translations and compliance documentation appropriate to the transaction.
Restricted AreasAcquisitions in designated border areas may require prior permission for certain foreign individuals or entities. The property location and purchaser status should be checked early.
Language ConsiderationsGreek is central to notarial, Cadastre, tax, planning and public processes. English may support commercial negotiations but does not replace Greek formal documentation.
International RulesEU rules, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, tax, financing and group-governance requirements may influence the wider transaction structure.
Typical RisksAssuming that property records are uniform nationwide, overlooking whether a Cadastre or legacy registry applies, or failing to assess construction, planning, forest, coastal or foreign-buyer restrictions before signing.

Operating Constraints and Risks

Registration-System RiskThe applicable property-registration authority may differ by location because the transition from historical land registries to the Hellenic Cadastre is not uniform in every area.
Title and Cadastre RiskIncomplete review of title chain, registry entries, cadastral boundaries, mortgages, easements and claims can leave material legal or technical issues unidentified.
Notarial and Tax RiskMissing tax certificates, property documentation, identity evidence, mortgage releases or other prerequisites can delay execution of the transfer deed.
Planning and Building RiskUnauthorised works, missing permits, incomplete building regularity, zoning restrictions, forest maps, coastal rules and archaeological controls can materially affect use and value.

Cross-Border RiskForeign identification, border-area restrictions, authority evidence, legalisation, translations, tax registration and anti-money-laundering controls can add time and formalities.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Hellenic Cadastre?The Hellenic Cadastre is Greece’s national property-registration system. In areas where it is operational, it records ownership, boundaries and legal rights relating to property.
Does the Cadastre apply across all of Greece?Not uniformly. Greece is transitioning from historical local land-registry systems to the national Cadastre. The applicable registration authority must be verified for the individual property.
Is a notary required to transfer Greek real property?Yes. The transfer of ownership is generally formalised through an authenticated notarial deed before a Greek notary public.
Why is registration after signing important?Registration or transcription of the deed at the competent Cadastre Office or local registry provides public notice and protects the acquired right against third parties.
Are foreign buyers restricted?Foreign purchasers can participate, but specific restrictions or permissions may apply in designated border areas and transaction-specific legal review is required.

Practical Guidance

Before acquiring, financing or developing Greek property, identify the applicable Cadastre or legacy land-registry system and obtain current legal and technical property information. The title chain, Cadastre mapping, tax status, building regularity, planning and environmental controls, notarial deed, registration, foreign-party documentation and financing should be managed as one coordinated transaction workstream.

Preparation checklist: Confirm property location, Cadastre code and competent registry; review title, ownership, mortgages, easements, claims and restrictions; compare cadastral and physical data; review tax, planning, forest, coastal, building and permit status; determine the transaction structure; verify foreign-buyer eligibility, signing authority and documentation; prepare the notarial deed; map tax, registration and financing actions; and align legal, tax, technical and financing workstreams.
