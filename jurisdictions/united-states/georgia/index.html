Executive Summary

Real estate law in Georgia is the State and local legal framework governing ownership, transfer, recording, financing, leasing, use and development of land and buildings. It operates through county Superior Court Clerk real-property records, Georgia property and contract law, local planning and zoning powers, and a closing process commonly coordinated by attorneys, title companies, lenders, settlement professionals and licensed real estate professionals.

A Georgia property transaction normally requires review of title evidence, county-recorded deeds, security deeds, liens, easements, restrictive covenants, leases, surveys, tax status, zoning and transaction documents. The seller executes a deed, commonly a limited warranty deed, warranty deed or quitclaim deed, which is delivered at closing and recorded with the Clerk of Superior Court in the county where the property is located. Recording provides public notice and is central to priority and third-party protection.

Georgia maintains county-level official land records through Clerks of Superior Court in all 159 counties. The Georgia Superior Court Clerks’ Cooperative Authority, GSCCCA, provides a statewide search system for real-property indexes and documents, including deed dockets, images and conveyance transfer-tax data. The county Clerk remains the local recording authority and custodian for instruments recorded in the county.

Georgia transfers are closely connected to real estate transfer tax and the PT-61 Real Estate Transfer Tax Declaration. Before a deed, security instrument or other qualifying writing is recorded, applicable transfer tax must be paid and the Clerk attaches or enters certification. The PT-61 is filed electronically through the GSCCCA process. Foreign seller transactions can also trigger federal FIRPTA withholding and require coordinated tax, entity, title, financing and recording analysis.

Georgia Characteristics

Georgia real estate is defined by county Superior Court Clerk recording, a statewide GSCCCA real-property records search system, transfer-tax clearance before qualifying recording, and local planning and zoning. The Clerk of Superior Court is responsible for filing, computer-indexing and permanently recording deeds, mortgages, liens, maps or plats and other instruments affecting real estate in the county.

County Recording EnvironmentThe Clerk of Superior Court in each Georgia county files, indexes and permanently records deeds, mortgages, liens, maps or plats and other instruments conveying, transferring, encumbering or affecting real property.
GSCCCA EnvironmentThe Georgia Superior Court Clerks’ Cooperative Authority provides a statewide real-property records system with access to deed docket indexes and images, PT-61 conveyance transfer-tax data and related real-property records for all Georgia counties.
Recording EffectGeorgia follows a notice-based recording framework. A deed is recorded in the Clerk of Superior Court’s office of the county where the land lies; a prior unrecorded deed can lose priority to a later recorded deed taken from the same vendor by a purchaser without notice.
Transfer Tax EnvironmentReal estate transfer tax is an excise tax on qualifying transfers of real property. Before a deed, security instrument or other qualifying writing is recorded, the transfer tax must be paid and the Clerk or deputy certifies payment.
PT-61 EnvironmentThe PT-61 Real Estate Transfer Tax Declaration is filed electronically through the GSCCCA process. It records transfer information and supports transfer-tax administration and recording documentation.
Security EnvironmentGeorgia real estate finance commonly uses a security deed securing a promissory note. Security deeds, assignments, cancellations and related lender instruments are recorded with the Clerk of Superior Court.
Conveyancing EnvironmentTransactions commonly use a purchase and sale agreement, title commitment, survey, attorney-led closing, PT-61 declaration, deed, security deed, promissory note, tax and recording documents.
Planning and Development EnvironmentCities and counties regulate land use through comprehensive plans, zoning, subdivision, plats, development permits, building permits, environmental review and local authority conditions. Rules vary substantially by local government and property location.
Language ExpectationEnglish is the standard language for deeds, title, closing, PT-61, Superior Court Clerk filing, tax, planning and court processes. Foreign documents may require notarisation, apostille, certification or translation acceptable to transaction parties and authorities.

Process Flow

A Georgia property transaction is a coordinated title, contract, attorney, closing, PT-61, transfer-tax, financing and Superior Court Clerk recording process. The property and local jurisdiction are identified, title and due diligence are completed, transaction and financing documents are negotiated, transfer tax is addressed, settlement occurs, and the deed and security deed are recorded with the Clerk of Superior Court.

1. County and Property IdentificationConfirm county, municipality or unincorporated area, tax parcel, legal description, title reference, zoning designation, association status and applicable Clerk or local authorities.
2. Title, GSCCCA, Survey and Property ReviewReview title commitment, Superior Court Clerk records, GSCCCA indexes and images, prior deeds, security deeds, cancellations, liens, easements, covenants, leases, plats, survey, property-tax status and recorded notices.
3. Wider Due DiligenceReview leases, condominium or association documents, zoning, comprehensive plan, permits, environmental matters, building condition, flood risk, utilities, tax, insurance and commercial risks.
4. Contract, Closing and Financing DocumentationNegotiate purchase and sale agreement, attorney closing requirements, financing and security documents, representations, warranties, disclosures, conditions and closing mechanics.
5. PT-61, Transfer Tax and SettlementPrepare and electronically file PT-61; determine transfer-tax amount, exemption and certification requirements; execute and deliver deed, loan documents, security deed and closing materials; complete payment, lien payoff, FIRPTA analysis, possession and settlement.
6. Recording and Follow-UpRecord the deed, security deed, cancellation, assignment, easement, plat or other instruments with the County Clerk of Superior Court; then complete title policy issuance, tax, lease, planning and post-closing actions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where are Georgia property deeds recorded?Deeds and other instruments affecting Georgia real property are filed and recorded with the Clerk of Superior Court in the county where the property is located.
What is GSCCCA?The Georgia Superior Court Clerks’ Cooperative Authority provides statewide access to real-property record indexes and documents, including deed dockets, images and PT-61 conveyance transfer-tax data, for all Georgia counties. The County Clerk remains the local recording authority.
What is PT-61?PT-61 is the Georgia Real Estate Transfer Tax Declaration. It is filed electronically through the GSCCCA process to report conveyance information and support transfer-tax administration and recording.
Can a Georgia deed be recorded before transfer tax is resolved?Generally no. Before a deed, security instrument or other qualifying writing can be recorded in the Clerk of Superior Court’s office, the applicable real estate transfer tax must be paid. The Clerk or deputy certifies payment as part of the process.
What is a security deed?A security deed is the common Georgia instrument used to secure a promissory note with real property. It is recorded with the Clerk of Superior Court and provides the lender with security rights under Georgia law.
Can foreign sellers trigger withholding?Yes. A foreign seller’s disposition of Georgia property can trigger federal FIRPTA withholding. PT-61, Georgia transfer-tax and any applicable financing or county recording requirements must also be completed before recording.

Practical Guidance

Before acquiring, financing or developing Georgia property, identify the county and municipality first, and confirm the relevant Clerk of Superior Court. Obtain a title commitment, GSCCCA and county Clerk records, survey, tax-assessor and tax information, zoning and permit records and, where applicable, condominium, association or environmental materials. The purchase agreement, attorney-led closing, deed, security deed, PT-61, real estate transfer tax, FIRPTA, Clerk recording, financing and local development controls should be managed as one coordinated workstream.

Preparation checklist: Identify the Georgia county and municipality; confirm Clerk of Superior Court recording route; obtain title commitment, GSCCCA and county land-record information, survey, plat, assessor, tax and PT-61 information; identify security deeds, liens, easements, restrictive covenants, leases, condominium or association obligations and restrictions; review comprehensive plan, zoning, subdivision, development, environmental, permit and building status; determine the transaction structure; verify signing authority and foreign-party documentation; electronically file PT-61 and confirm taxable or exempt treatment; assess FIRPTA and intangible recording tax where relevant; prepare closing, deed, financing, tax and recording materials; and align legal, tax, technical, planning, title and financing workstreams.
