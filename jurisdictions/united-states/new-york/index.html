Executive Summary

Real estate law in New York is the State and local legal framework governing ownership, transfer, recording, financing, leasing, use and development of land and buildings. It operates through county-level recording systems, New York State property and contract law, local land-use regulation and a conveyancing process commonly coordinated by attorneys, title companies, lenders and, in New York City, the City Register system.

A New York property transaction normally requires review of title records, deeds, mortgages, liens, easements, leases, surveys, tax status, zoning and transaction documents. The seller executes a deed, commonly a bargain and sale deed with covenant against grantor’s acts, which is delivered at closing and recorded with the County Clerk or City Register where the property is located. Recording provides public notice and is central to priority and third-party protection.

New York does not operate a single statewide land register. Recording is handled through county-level offices. In New York City, the Office of the City Register records property documents in the Bronx, Brooklyn, Manhattan and Queens through the Automated City Register Information System, ACRIS; property records for Staten Island are maintained through the Richmond County Clerk. Other counties operate through the applicable County Clerk or County Register system.

Cross-border transactions require coordination of New York title, recording, escrow, tax, foreign seller withholding, beneficial ownership, land-use and local compliance requirements. Foreign buyers can generally acquire New York property, but a foreign seller’s disposition can trigger federal FIRPTA withholding, and nonresident sellers can be subject to New York State withholding requirements. The county, municipality, property type and investor structure should be determined before closing.

Purpose

The purpose of the real-estate law function is to establish a reliable legal basis for acquiring, holding, financing, using, leasing or developing New York property. It converts commercial terms into effective conveyancing documentation, recorded interests and a property position that can be supported by title evidence and title insurance where applicable.

For an investor, owner or occupier, this requires analysis of title records, deeds, mortgages, liens, easements, restrictive covenants, leases, cooperative or condominium documents, zoning, permits, environmental issues, property taxes, transfer taxes and the correct county or New York City recording route.

New York Characteristics

New York real estate is defined by decentralised recording and locally administered land use. County Clerks and County Registers record real property instruments across the State, while New York City uses a specialised City Register and ACRIS infrastructure for most boroughs. Attorney-led transactions, title insurance and detailed title searches are common features of the State’s property-transfer environment.

County Recording EnvironmentNew York deeds, mortgages and other instruments affecting real property are generally recorded with the County Clerk or County Register in the county where the property is located.
New York City Recording EnvironmentThe NYC Office of the City Register records and maintains real property documents in Manhattan, Bronx, Brooklyn and Queens through ACRIS. Staten Island property records are handled through the Richmond County Clerk.
ACRIS EnvironmentACRIS allows public search of recorded property documents and document images for the four covered boroughs. It provides access to deeds, mortgages, liens, satisfactions and related records from 1966 onward.
Recording EffectNew York follows a race-notice recording approach. A conveyance that is duly acknowledged or proved may be recorded in the county where the property is situated, and priority can depend on recording sequence and purchaser notice.
Conveyancing EnvironmentNew York transactions are commonly attorney-led. The process typically includes contract negotiation, title and survey review, lender coordination, closing, deed delivery, transfer-tax filing and post-closing recording.
Title Insurance EnvironmentTitle companies and title insurers conduct searches of recorded public documents and commonly issue title commitments and policies for owners and lenders, subject to policy exceptions and requirements.
Planning EnvironmentLand-use and development controls are administered by cities, towns, villages and counties under New York State enabling legislation. New York City has a separate zoning, building and land-use environment involving the Department of City Planning and Department of Buildings.
Language ExpectationEnglish is the standard language for deeds, title, escrow, recording, tax, planning and court processes. Foreign documents may require notarisation, apostille, certification or translation acceptable to the relevant parties, title insurer, lender or recording office.

Process Flow

A New York property transaction is a coordinated title, contract, attorney, financing, tax and county-recording process. The property and recording office are identified, title and due diligence are completed, the contract and financing documentation are negotiated, the parties close, and the deed and security instruments are recorded with the County Clerk, County Register or City Register.

1. County and Property IdentificationConfirm the county, city, town or village, tax parcel or borough-block-lot reference, legal description, title reference, zoning designation and applicable recording or planning authorities.
2. Title, Survey and Property ReviewReview title report or commitment, recorded deeds, mortgages, liens, easements, covenants, leases, maps, survey, tax status, notices and other local property records.
3. Wider Due DiligenceReview leases, zoning, comprehensive plans, permits, environmental matters, building condition, utilities, tax, insurance, condominium or cooperative records and commercial risks.
4. Contract, Escrow and Financing DocumentationNegotiate contract of sale, escrow arrangements, financing and security documents, representations, warranties, indemnities, disclosure materials, conditions and closing mechanics.
5. Closing and SettlementExecute and deliver the deed, loan documents, mortgage and closing materials; complete payment, lien payoff, transfer taxes, possession and agreed settlement actions.
6. Recording and Follow-UpRecord the deed, mortgage, satisfaction, assignment or other instruments with the relevant County Clerk, County Register or City Register; then complete title policy, tax, lease and post-closing actions.

Decision Tree

  1. In which New York county and municipality is the property located?
  2. Is the property in New York City, and if so, is it in Manhattan, Bronx, Brooklyn, Queens or Staten Island?
  3. Which recording office applies: County Clerk, County Register, City Register through ACRIS or Richmond County Clerk?
  4. What do the title report, recorded documents, tax parcel information, survey or map show about ownership, mortgages, liens, easements, covenants, leases and exceptions?
  5. Is the property a fee-simple parcel, condominium unit, cooperative interest, commercial property, development site, agricultural property or subject to another specialised regime?
  6. Do local zoning, subdivision rules, SEQRA, site plan, landmark, environmental, building permit or development requirements affect the intended use?
  7. Which contract, deed, transfer tax forms, mortgage, assignment, lien release, escrow, title insurance and recording documents are required?
  8. Does the transaction involve a foreign seller, foreign buyer, foreign entity, FIRPTA, New York withholding, sanctions, entity disclosure, financing or other cross-border requirements?

Frequently Asked Questions

Where are New York property deeds recorded?Deeds and other instruments affecting New York real property are generally recorded with the County Clerk or County Register in the county where the property is located. In Manhattan, Bronx, Brooklyn and Queens, documents are recorded by the New York City City Register through ACRIS; Staten Island uses the Richmond County Clerk.
What is ACRIS?ACRIS is the Automated City Register Information System used by New York City to search property records, view document images and create recording cover pages and tax forms for Manhattan, Bronx, Brooklyn and Queens.
Does recording guarantee title?No. Recording provides public notice and affects priority, but title must still be investigated through recorded documents and title searches. Title insurance is commonly used to insure against specified title risks, subject to policy terms and exceptions.
What type of deed is commonly used in New York?A bargain and sale deed with covenant against grantor’s acts is commonly used, although the appropriate deed form depends on the transaction, seller, title condition and legal advice.
Are planning and building permits State or local?Planning, zoning, subdivision, development and building permits are primarily administered by cities, towns, villages and counties under New York State law. New York City has its own zoning and building approval system.
Can foreign sellers trigger withholding?Yes. Foreign sellers can trigger federal FIRPTA withholding, while nonresident sellers can also trigger New York State real estate transfer withholding. The applicable position should be confirmed before closing.

Practical Guidance

Before acquiring, financing or developing New York property, identify the county and municipality first and confirm whether the property is within New York City. Obtain a title report, recorded documents, survey or tax-map information, zoning and permit records and, where appropriate, cooperative or condominium materials. The contract, attorney review, escrow, deed, mortgage, transfer taxes, withholding, recording, financing and local development controls should be managed as one coordinated workstream.

Preparation checklist: Identify the New York county and municipality; confirm City Register, ACRIS, County Clerk or County Register route; obtain title, recorded document and survey or tax-map information; identify mortgages, liens, easements, covenants, leases, cooperative or condominium obligations and restrictions; review zoning, planning, permits, SEQRA and building status; determine the transaction structure; verify signing authority and foreign-party documentation; assess FIRPTA and New York withholding where relevant; prepare contract, closing, deed, tax, financing and recording materials; and align legal, tax, technical, planning, title and financing workstreams.
